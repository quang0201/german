# Design Research

Research before drawing when the brief does not already provide a visual target.

## Research workflow

1. Identify the product category, user, primary task, and emotional/brand lane.
2. Inspect the existing product and design system if any.
3. When browsing is available, collect 4–8 current references that are relevant by product behavior or visual problem, not merely attractive.
4. Separate:
   - direct product references;
   - interaction/pattern references;
   - typography/layout references;
   - atmospheric references.
5. Build an anti-board: identify 3–5 directions that would be wrong for this product and why.
6. Extract decisions instead of copying surfaces:
   - information hierarchy;
   - layout geometry;
   - density;
   - type relationships;
   - color roles;
   - component shapes;
   - motion;
   - imagery;
   - states and feedback.
7. Commit to one primary direction. Do not blend every reference.
8. Write or update `DESIGN.md` using the bundled template when the project lacks a design contract.

## Research standard

A reference is useful only if it changes a concrete design decision.

Do not report "inspired by Stripe/Linear/Apple" without specifying what is being borrowed and why it fits the product.

Prefer current, real product screenshots and official product pages over generic template galleries when possible.

If external browsing is unavailable, say so and use:
- supplied references;
- the existing product;
- existing brand/design-system files.

Do not fabricate trend research.

## Deliverable

Produce a compact research log:

- Product/audience/task
- 4–8 references with one useful observation each
- 3–5 anti-references / rejected conventions
- Chosen design thesis in one paragraph
- Signature move
- Typography direction
- Color strategy
- Layout/density strategy
- Interaction/motion strategy
- Risks and constraints

Read `references/visual-contract.md` before finalizing the direction.
